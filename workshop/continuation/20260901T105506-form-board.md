## Live deliberations

- **How `Board.Prompt()` renders is undecided in detail.** The mock shows four
  columns at fixed width with `[label] word`. Real words vary from `keel` to
  `arrondissement`, and the plan does not say whether columns are fixed-width
  (simple, wastes space, may clip a long word) or ragged (packs better, harder to
  click-map). Leaning fixed-width with the width derived from the longest word in
  *that* board, and truncation only when even that will not fit 80 columns.
- **Whether `Mark` is exported.** The loop needs to hand a click to the board,
  which means a method taking a cell index and a mark. If `Mark` is exported,
  `main` can name it and `play` has widened its public surface for one caller. The
  alternative is that a click arrives as a `play.Input` with a cell index —
  keeping the surface closed but growing `Input`. Not resolved; D13 set the
  precedent that "what is being answered" belongs in `play`, which argues for the
  second.

## Lessons learned

- **A parked branch's issue file is the current one; `main`'s copy is as old as
  the park.** Resuming `#38` I read the issue on `main` and re-derived its
  estimate from a 2.18 block the post-T0 session had already superseded with a
  considered 2.83. Check out the branch before reading the record.
- **`git log --grep` is branch-local.** I told the operator `#38` had never been
  started, from a grep run while sitting on `#41`'s branch. It had a full task
  done on its own branch.
- **A scripted edit must assert on its anchor.** Ticking seven plan rows silently
  did nothing because the anchor text had already changed; the gate found them
  unticked two rounds later. A `replace` that matches nothing is
  indistinguishable from one that worked.
- **The surfaces a revision must sweep are not three but "every artifact that
  restates the decision".** I swept the plan's prose, tables and Done-when — and
  the gate found the *issue's* Done-when still naming a threshold nothing used.
- **When two things must agree about a measurement, one owns it and the other
  asks.** Both of `#38`'s Criticals were two owners of one number: the loop's
  width against the screen's, and the wrap against the map.
